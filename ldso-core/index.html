This document specifies LDSO-Core, the Core Module of the Legal Document Structure Ontology (LDSO). LDSO-Core provides a generic, modular RDF/OWL vocabulary for representing the structural hierarchy, ordering, and identity of legal documents including treaties, trade agreements, laws, regulations, contracts, insurance policies, standards, judicial decisions, and parliamentary acts.

LDSO-Core covers document structure only. Legal semantics (obligations, permissions, definitions), legal lifecycle (amendments, versioning), and domain-specific operational rules belong to separate LDSO modules.

The ontology namespace is https://w3id.org/ldso/core# (preferred prefix: ldso).

Introduction

Purpose

Every legal instrument — a trade agreement, a statute, a contract, a judicial decision — is built from the same structural grammar. A treaty has a preamble, a body divided into chapters, chapters divided into articles, articles divided into numbered paragraphs, paragraphs introducing lettered lists, lists containing items, items containing further structure. This grammar is not decoration: it is the primary addressing system by which legal practitioners, courts, automated systems, and knowledge graphs navigate, cite, and reason about legal text.

LDSO-Core provides a generic, graph-native RDF/OWL vocabulary for representing that structural grammar. It answers the question: where is this text, and how does it relate to the other text around it? — without saying anything about what the text means legally.

Structure and meaning are kept strictly separate. The class ldso:Article records that a node is a primary provision unit at a given position in a document. Whether that article imposes an obligation, grants a permission, or provides a definition is the concern of LDSO-Semantics, a higher-layer module. LDSO-Core never encodes legal meaning.

Problems This Specification Solves

Structural fragmentation across corpora

A trade lawyer working across the CPTPP, the EU-Mercosur Agreement, and a domestic regulation encounters three different document structures, three different numbering schemes, and three different digital formats. No common graph vocabulary exists to represent all three uniformly so that a single SPARQL query can retrieve, say, all articles in all three instruments that deal with temporary admission.

LDSO-Core solves this by providing instrument-neutral classes such as ldso:Article, ldso:Paragraph, and ldso:Item that apply across jurisdictions and document types. The notation "2.5" is preserved in ldso:notation regardless of whether the source is a treaty, a regulation, or an insurance policy.

Ordering without RDF Collections

RDF Collections (rdf:List) represent ordered sequences but are cumbersome for large legal corpora: they cannot be extended without rewriting the list, they do not support amendment insertion, and they do not support bidirectional traversal or index-based lookup in SPARQL.

LDSO-Core solves this with a doubly-linked list pattern: ldso:next and ldso:previous chain siblings, and ldso:orderIndex provides an integer for ORDER BY queries. When a new paragraph is inserted by amendment, only two pointer updates are required; no renumbering of the list is needed. 

# Before amendment: para_1 → para_2
:para_1  ldso:next :para_2 ; ldso:orderIndex 1 .
:para_2  ldso:previous :para_1 ; ldso:orderIndex 2 .

# After inserting para_1bis between para_1 and para_2:
:para_1     ldso:next :para_1bis ; ldso:orderIndex 1 .
:para_1bis  ldso:previous :para_1 ; ldso:next :para_2 ; ldso:orderIndex 2 .
:para_2     ldso:previous :para_1bis ; ldso:orderIndex 3 .

Stable element identity and multilingual versions

A provision cited in a legal brief must remain identifiable even after the document is amended. LDSO-Core uses a graph-native identity model: the node IRI is the primary identity for every legal element. ldso:notation carries the local label as printed in the source document (e.g., "5"), and ldso:citation carries the full concatenated citation path (e.g., "2.5").

At the document level, LDSO-Core separates the abstract instrument from its language versions using ldso:LegalResource and ldso:LegalExpression. The English and French texts of a treaty are two ldso:LegalExpression nodes linked to the same ldso:LegalResource. Bibliographic metadata (dct:title, dct:language, dct:issued) is carried by the LegalExpression, not by custom LDSO properties. 

<https://example.org/resource/ta>
  a ldso:LegalResource ;
  dct:title "Agreement on Temporary Admission"@en ;
  ldso:hasExpression <https://example.org/doc/ta/EN> ,
                     <https://example.org/doc/ta/FR> .

<https://example.org/doc/ta/EN>
  a ldso:Document ;
  dct:title "Agreement on Temporary Admission"@en ;
  dct:language "en" ;
  dct:issued "2026-01-15"^^xsd:date ;
  ldso:expressionOf <https://example.org/resource/ta> .

<https://example.org/doc/ta/EN/body/chp_2/art_5>
  ldso:notation "5" ;   # local label as printed
  ldso:citation "2.5" . # dot-concatenated path from document root

Inline cross-references within text

Legal text is dense with cross-references: "as referred to in paragraph 2", "subject to Article 5.3", "as defined in Annex I". These references are embedded in prose but must be traversable as graph edges for citation analysis, impact assessment, and question answering.

LDSO-Core solves this by modelling each inline reference as a ldso:InternalReference or ldso:ExternalReference node contained by the citing element. The property ldso:anchorText records the surface phrase; ldso:refersTo points to the target node. 

:para_1
  ldso:text "...goods referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article..."@en ;
  ldso:contains :para_1/ref_1 .

:para_1/ref_1
  a ldso:InternalReference ;
  ldso:anchorText "paragraph 2 of this Article"@en ;
  ldso:refersTo   :para_2 .

Multilingual text on the same structural node

International instruments are authenticated in multiple languages, all equally authoritative. LDSO-Core uses rdf:langString for ldso:text, allowing multiple language variants to be asserted on the same node without structural duplication. 

:art_2p5/para_1
  ldso:text "Each Party shall allow the temporary admission..."@en ;
  ldso:text "Chaque Partie autorise l'admission temporaire..."@fr ;
  ldso:text "Cada Parte permitirá la admisión temporal..."@es .

Module Architecture and Layer Relationships

LDSO-Core is the foundation layer of the Legal Document Structure Ontology family. Each layer above it imports and extends the layers below. The separation is enforced as a hard architectural constraint: no concept from a higher layer may appear in a lower-layer instance.

LayerModuleResponsibilityDepends on
6 Evaluation Rule execution results, compliance scores, gap analysis outputs Layer 5
5 Domain ontologies
(Trade Agreement Ontology, Insurance Ontology…)		 Operational rules: tariff rules, origin rules, document requirements, policy conditions Layer 4
4 LDSO-Semantics Legal meaning: obligations, permissions, prohibitions, exceptions, definitions, conditions Layer 3
3 LDSO-Lifecycle Legal change: amendments, repeal, consolidation, versioning, entry into force Layers 1 + 2
2b LDSO-Entities Named entity annotation: persons, organizations, locations, concepts, roles, events, processes Layer 1
1 LDSO-Core ← this specification Legal resource and expression identity, document structure, hierarchy, ordering, text content, references —

The key invariant: structure tells where text is. Semantics tells what text means. Lifecycle tells how text changes. Domain rules tell how to operationalise the meaning. These concerns must never be mixed in a single layer.

A correct multi-layer instance looks like this: 

# Layer 1 — LDSO-Core: where the text is
:art_2p5
  a ldso:Article ;
  ldso:notation "2.5" ;
  dct:title "Temporary Admission of Goods"@en .

# Layer 4 — LDSO-Semantics: what the text means (separate graph)
:obligation_art2p5_para1
  a leg:Obligation ;
  leg:derivedFrom :art_2p5/para_1 ;
  leg:subject     :Party ;
  leg:modality    leg:MUST .

# Layer 5 — Domain: operational rule (separate graph)
:rule_temporaryAdmission
  a trade:CustomsProcedureRule ;
  trade:sourceProvision :art_2p5 ;
  trade:modality trade:MUST_NOT_COLLECT_DUTY .

The structural node :art_2p5 is the anchor. Higher layers point to it; they do not modify it.

Class Guide — Rationale and Usage

The following sections explain each class: why it exists as a distinct class, when to use it, and a concrete example.

ldso:LegalResource

The root of the class hierarchy. Anything that can be cited or referenced in a legal graph is a LegalResource — a complete document, an element within a document, or an external reference target. It exists to give ldso:refersTo and ldso:cites a typed range that covers both documents and elements without forcing them into the same class.

In practice you will never instantiate ldso:LegalResource directly — use ldso:Document or one of the ldso:LegalElement subclasses.

ldso:Document

A complete legal instrument. It is a ldso:LegalResource but deliberately not a ldso:LegalElement — documents contain elements, they are not contained by them. 

<https://example.org/doc/cptpp/EN>
  a ldso:Document ;
  dct:title "Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership"@en ;
  dct:language "en" ;
  dct:issued "2018-12-30"^^xsd:date ;
  ldso:contains <.../preamble> , <.../body> , <.../conclusions> .

ldso:Preface, ldso:Preamble, ldso:Body, ldso:Conclusions

These are the four top-level containers within a document. They exist as distinct classes because they have different legal functions: the Preamble states motivation (and is used in treaty interpretation); the Body contains operative provisions; the Conclusions contain authentication. Distinguishing them allows queries such as "retrieve all preamble recitals that mention climate" without scanning the entire document. 

<.../preamble>
  a ldso:Preamble ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:contains <.../preamble/recital_1> , <.../preamble/recital_2> .

<.../body>
  a ldso:Body ;
  ldso:orderIndex 2 ;
  ldso:contains <.../body/chp_1> , <.../body/chp_2> .

ldso:Part, ldso:Title, ldso:Chapter, ldso:Section

These are the hierarchical division classes. Each exists because different legal traditions use different levels: common law statutes typically use Part → Section; civil law instruments use Part → Title → Chapter → Section. Not every instrument uses every level. Use only the levels that appear in the source document.

There is no separate ldso:Subsection class. A Section contained within another Section occupies the "subsection" position — that role is determined by the ldso:partOf graph edge, not by type assignment. Creating a subclass would be anti-rigid: the same node could be a Section in one context and a Subsection in another. 

<.../body/chp_2>
  a ldso:Chapter ;
  ldso:notation "2" ;
  dct:title "Customs Facilitation"@en ;
  ldso:orderIndex 2 ;
  ldso:next <.../body/chp_3> .

ldso:Article

The primary normative provision unit in treaties and regulations. Articles are the unit at which legal practitioners cite: "Article 2.5 of the Agreement". They are also the natural unit for legal semantic annotation by LDSO-Semantics.

Use ldso:Article when the source document uses "Article" as the primary provision term. Use ldso:Clause for contracts and standards that use "clause" instead. 

<.../chp_2/art_5>
  a ldso:Article ;
  ldso:notation "5" ;
  ldso:citation "2.5" ;
  dct:title "Temporary Admission of Goods"@en ;
  ldso:orderIndex 5 ;
  ldso:previous <.../chp_2/art_4> ;
  ldso:next     <.../chp_2/art_6> .

ldso:Paragraph

A numbered or lettered subdivision of an Article. Paragraphs are the most frequently cited sub-article unit: "Article 2.5, paragraph 2". They may contain further text, BlockLists, Tables, or nested Paragraphs.

There is no separate ldso:Subparagraph class. The structural position conventionally called "subparagraph" is a ldso:Paragraph whose ldso:partOf points to another ldso:Paragraph. This is a positional role determined by the containment graph, not an intrinsic property of the node — creating a subclass would produce an anti-rigid class that violates sound ontological design. 

<.../art_5/para_2>
  a ldso:Paragraph ;
  ldso:notation "2" ;
  ldso:citation "2.5.2" ;
  ldso:orderIndex 2 ;
  ldso:text "The following goods shall be eligible..."@en ;
  ldso:previous <.../art_5/para_1> ;
  ldso:next     <.../art_5/para_3> .

ldso:Clause

The equivalent of ldso:Article for instruments that use "clause" as the primary provision term: contracts, commercial agreements, insurance policies, technical standards. The class distinction allows queries that scope to a specific document type without relying on document metadata. 

<.../policy/clause_4p2>
  a ldso:Clause ;
  ldso:notation "4.2" ;
  dct:title "Exclusions"@en ;
  ldso:orderIndex 2 .

ldso:BlockList and ldso:Item

Legal provisions frequently enumerate conditions, exceptions, or eligible categories as lettered or numbered lists. A ldso:BlockList is the container; each ldso:Item is one entry. Items are ordered via ldso:next, ldso:previous, and ldso:orderIndex.

There is no separate ldso:Subpoint class for the same reason there is no ldso:Subparagraph: a "subpoint" is a ldso:Point contained within another ldso:Point, identified by the graph structure alone. 

<.../para_2/list_1>
  a ldso:BlockList ;
  ldso:contains
    <.../list_1/item_a> ,
    <.../list_1/item_b> ,
    <.../list_1/item_c> .

<.../list_1/item_a>
  a ldso:Item ;
  ldso:notation "a" ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:text "goods for display at exhibitions;"@en ;
  ldso:next <.../list_1/item_b> .

ldso:Annex, ldso:Schedule, ldso:Appendix, ldso:Attachment

Supplements to the main body. They are distinct classes because they have different legal status: Annexes are typically legally binding and form an integral part of the instrument; Schedules are data tables (tariff rates, product lists); Appendices are typically explanatory; Attachments are generic exhibits. Using the correct class allows downstream modules to apply the right legal treatment. 

<.../annex_I>
  a ldso:Annex ;
  ldso:notation "I" ;
  dct:title "Tariff Elimination Schedule — Party A"@en ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:contains <.../annex_I/table_1> .

ldso:Table, ldso:Row, ldso:Cell

Tabular data is pervasive in legal documents: tariff rate tables, compliance matrices, schedules of commitments. The table model is recursive: a ldso:Cell may contain a nested ldso:Table via ldso:hasTable, and a ldso:Row may contain sub-rows via ldso:hasRow. Both ldso:hasRow and ldso:hasCell are sub-properties of ldso:contains and have domain ldso:LegalElement to support this recursion.

A row that serves as a column-header row is identified by asserting ldso:isHeader true. This is a datatype property, not a subclass, because "header" is a structural role determined by position, not an intrinsic property of the row. 

<.../table_1>
  a ldso:Table ;
  ldso:hasRow <.../table_1/row_1> , <.../table_1/row_2> .

<.../table_1/row_1>
  a ldso:Row ;
  ldso:isHeader true ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:next <.../table_1/row_2> ;
  ldso:hasCell <.../row_1/cell_1> , <.../row_1/cell_2> .

<.../row_1/cell_1>
  a ldso:Cell ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:text "HS Code"@en .

ldso:Reference, ldso:InternalReference, ldso:ExternalReference, ldso:Citation

Legal text is connected text. Every provision that says "as provided in Article 5" or "subject to the WTO Agreement" creates a navigable edge in the legal graph. LDSO-Core reifies these edges as first-class nodes so that reference metadata — the surface phrase, the target, the type of reference — can be stored and queried.

An ldso:InternalReference points to another element within the same document. An ldso:ExternalReference points to a resource in a different document. A ldso:Citation is a formal citation of a legal authority or precedent. All three require ldso:anchorText (the surface phrase in the source text) and ldso:refersTo or ldso:cites (the graph target). 

<.../para_1>
  ldso:text "...referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article..."@en ;
  ldso:contains <.../para_1/ref_1> .

<.../para_1/ref_1>
  a ldso:InternalReference ;
  ldso:anchorText "paragraph 2 of this Article"@en ;
  ldso:refersTo   <.../para_2> .

<.../para_3>
  ldso:text "...in accordance with the WTO Agreement on Customs Valuation..."@en ;
  ldso:contains <.../para_3/ref_1> .

<.../para_3/ref_1>
  a ldso:ExternalReference ;
  ldso:anchorText "the WTO Agreement on Customs Valuation"@en ;
  ldso:refersTo   <https://www.wto.org/instruments/customs-valuation> .

Scope

In Scope

  • Document-level containers: Preface, Preamble, Body, Conclusions
  • Hierarchical divisions: Part, Title, Chapter, Section (subsection position is emergent from containment, not a class)
  • Provision units: Article, Paragraph, Clause (subparagraph position is emergent from containment, not a class)
  • List elements: Point, BlockList, Item (subpoint position is emergent from containment, not a class)
  • Supplements: Annex, Schedule, Appendix, Attachment
  • Table structure: Table, Row, Cell — recursive (nested tables, sub-rows, header rows)
  • Structural references: Reference, InternalReference, ExternalReference, Citation
  • Element ordering, multilingual text content, inline reference anchors, and element identity

Out of Scope

  • Legal semantics: obligations, permissions, prohibitions, exceptions — see LDSO-Semantics
  • Legal lifecycle: amendments, versions, consolidation — see LDSO-Lifecycle
  • Named entity annotation: persons, organizations, locations, concepts — see LDSO-Entities
  • Trade operational rules: tariff rules, origin rules — see Trade Agreement Ontology
  • SHACL execution semantics or rule evaluation results

Competency Questions

The following natural-language questions define the scope of what LDSO-Core is designed to answer. Each is accompanied by a representative SPARQL query against a conformant dataset. All queries assume the namespace prefix declarations from § Conventions.

CQ-C-01 — Which structural elements are contained in a given chapter?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>

SELECT ?element ?type ?citation WHERE {
  ?chapter a ldso:Chapter ;
           ldso:notation "3" .
  ?chapter ldso:contains+ ?element .
  ?element a ?type ;
           ldso:citation ?citation .
  FILTER(?type != owl:NamedIndividual)
}
ORDER BY ?citation

CQ-C-02 — What is the citation path of a given element?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>

SELECT ?citation ?notation ?title WHERE {
  <https://example.org/ata/art_5_para_2>
    ldso:citation ?citation ;
    ldso:notation ?notation .
  OPTIONAL { <https://example.org/ata/art_5_para_2> ldso:title ?title }
}

CQ-C-03 — Which element comes immediately before or after a given article?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>

SELECT ?prev ?next WHERE {
  OPTIONAL { <https://example.org/ata/art_5> ldso:previous ?prev . }
  OPTIONAL { <https://example.org/ata/art_5> ldso:next     ?next . }
}

CQ-C-04 — Which language expressions are available for a given legal resource?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>
PREFIX dct:  <http://purl.org/dc/terms/>

SELECT ?expression ?language ?issued WHERE {
  <https://example.org/ata> ldso:hasExpression ?expression .
  ?expression dct:language ?language .
  OPTIONAL { ?expression dct:issued ?issued }
}
ORDER BY ?language

CQ-C-05 — What is the top-level structural outline of a document?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>

SELECT ?element ?type ?notation ?orderIndex WHERE {
  <https://example.org/ata/body> ldso:contains ?element .
  ?element a ?type ;
           ldso:orderIndex ?orderIndex .
  OPTIONAL { ?element ldso:notation ?notation }
  FILTER(?type != owl:NamedIndividual)
}
ORDER BY ?orderIndex

CQ-C-06 — Are there broken next/previous chain links (integrity check)?

PREFIX ldso: <https://w3id.org/ldso/core#>

SELECT ?a ?b WHERE {
  ?a ldso:next ?b .
  FILTER NOT EXISTS { ?b ldso:previous ?a . }
}
# Expected result: no rows in a valid graph.

Conformance

A conforming LDSO-Core instance MUST:

  1. Use the namespace https://w3id.org/ldso/core# for all LDSO-Core terms.
  2. Identify every node by its IRI as the primary identity. Use ldso:notation for the local label and ldso:citation for the full concatenated citation path. Use dct:title, dct:language, and dct:issued for document metadata. Link ldso:LegalExpression nodes to their parent ldso:LegalResource via ldso:expressionOf.
  3. Express sibling ordering using ldso:next, ldso:previous, and ldso:orderIndex. RDF Collections (rdf:List) MUST NOT be used for ordering.
  4. Maintain chain integrity: if A ldso:next B, then B ldso:previous A MUST also hold.
  5. Not place legal semantics, obligations, permissions, or operational rules in LDSO-Core instances.
  6. Pass all SHACL shapes defined in shapes.ttl without violations.

There is no separate ldso:Subparagraph class. The structural position conventionally called "subparagraph" is a ldso:Paragraph whose ldso:partOf points to another ldso:Paragraph. The sub-position is an emergent property of the graph, not a rigid classification.

Datatype Property Definitions

Property: ldso:citation

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#citation
DefinitionThe full human-readable citation path to a legal element, formed by concatenating ldso:notation values from the document root to the element and joining them with dots.
Domainldso:LegalElement
Rangexsd:string
Usage noteExamples: "2" (chapter), "2.5" (article), "2.5.1" (paragraph), "2.5.2.a" (item). Validated by ldso:CitationChainShape.

Property: ldso:orderIndex

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#orderIndex
DefinitionAn integer indicating the sequential position of a legal element among its siblings (1-based).
Rangexsd:integer
Usage noteUse together with ldso:next and ldso:previous. Non-consecutive values are allowed to support amendment insertion.

Property: ldso:notation

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#notation
DefinitionThe human-readable identifier or label of a legal element as it appears in the source document.
Rangexsd:string
Usage noteThe local label at this structural level as printed in the source: "2", "5", "a", "I", "IV", "bis". Preserve exact source notation. For the full citation path, use ldso:citation.

Property: ldso:text

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#text
DefinitionThe verbatim text content of a legal element as it appears in the authoritative source.
Rangerdf:langString
Usage note Always assert with a BCP 47 language tag (@en, @fr, etc.). In RDF, a language-tagged literal has type rdf:langString, not xsd:string; the two are incompatible. Multiple translations of the same element may be represented as multiple ldso:text values with different language tags on the same node. 
:art_2p5/para_1
  ldso:text "Each Party shall allow the temporary admission of goods..."@en ;
  ldso:text "Chaque Partie autorise l'admission temporaire des marchandises..."@fr .

Property: ldso:anchorText

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#anchorText
DefinitionThe surface form of a reference as it appears in the text of the containing element — the exact phrase that constitutes the reference anchor.
Domainldso:Reference
Rangerdf:langString
Usage note Bridges the flat ldso:text literal and the structured ldso:refersTo link. Records which phrase in the source text triggers the reference without requiring character offsets. Always assert with a language tag.

The full inline reference pattern: the containing element holds the full text in ldso:text, and ldso:contains one or more ldso:Reference nodes. Each Reference carries ldso:anchorText for the surface phrase and ldso:refersTo for the target. 

:art_2p5/para_1
  a ldso:Paragraph ;
  ldso:text """Each Party shall allow the temporary admission of goods
referred to in paragraph 2 of this Article without payment of
customs duties and taxes."""@en ;
  ldso:contains :art_2p5/para_1/ref_1 .

:art_2p5/para_1/ref_1
  a ldso:InternalReference ;
  ldso:anchorText "paragraph 2 of this Article"@en ;
  ldso:refersTo   :art_2p5/para_2 .

For an external reference to another instrument:

:art_3/para_1/ref_1
  a ldso:ExternalReference ;
  ldso:anchorText "the WTO Agreement on Customs Valuation"@en ;
  ldso:refersTo   <https://www.wto.org/instruments/customs-valuation> .

Property: ldso:isHeader

IRIhttps://w3id.org/ldso/core#isHeader
DefinitionIndicates that a Row serves as a column-header row within its containing Table.
Domainldso:Row
Rangexsd:boolean
Usage note A header row is a structural role, not a distinct class — asserting ldso:isHeader true on a ldso:Row records the role without creating an anti-rigid subclass. Absence of the property means the row is a data row. Applies equally to header rows in nested tables. 
:table_1/row_1
  a ldso:Row ;
  ldso:isHeader true ;
  ldso:orderIndex 1 ;
  ldso:hasCell :table_1/row_1/cell_1 ,
               :table_1/row_1/cell_2 .

:table_1/row_2
  a ldso:Row ;
  ldso:orderIndex 2 ;
  ldso:previous :table_1/row_1 ;
  ldso:hasCell :table_1/row_2/cell_1 ,
               :table_1/row_2/cell_2 .

SPARQL to retrieve header and data rows separately:

PREFIX ldso: 

SELECT ?row ?idx WHERE {
  :table_1 ldso:hasRow ?row .
  ?row ldso:orderIndex ?idx .
  FILTER NOT EXISTS { ?row ldso:isHeader true }
}
ORDER BY ?idx

Alignment with Existing Standards

StandardRelationship
Akoma Ntoso [[AKN]] Structural element hierarchy inspired by AKN element taxonomy (article, paragraph, blocklist, annex, table). LDSO-Core is not an AKN ontology and does not use AKN's FRBRuri or eId/wId identifier scheme.
FRBR [[FRBR]] ldso:LegalResource ≈ FRBR Work; ldso:LegalExpression ≈ FRBR Expression; ldso:Document ≈ FRBR Manifestation. LDSO-Core uses a simplified two-level Resource/Expression model without the full FRBR hierarchy.
ELI [[ELI]] ELI IRIs can be used as ldso:LegalResource or ldso:LegalExpression identifiers. LDSO-Core is compatible with ELI metadata at the expression level.
Dublin Core / DCTerms dct:title, dct:language, dct:issued, and dct:format are the standard metadata properties used on ldso:LegalExpression and ldso:Document nodes. ldso:expressionOf is a sub-property of dct:isVersionOf.
SKOS [[SKOS]] skos:definition and skos:scopeNote are used throughout LDSO-Core for class and property documentation.
OWL 2 [[OWL2]] All classes and properties are declared as OWL terms. The ontology uses a limited OWL 2 DL subset compatible with open-world reasoning for large corpora.

Security Considerations

LDSO-Core is a descriptive ontology. It does not specify executable rules, access control policies, or encryption requirements. Implementors who expose LDSO-Core data via SPARQL endpoints or REST APIs MUST apply standard web security controls including authentication, authorisation, input validation, and rate limiting. Sensitive legal documents SHOULD be protected with appropriate access controls before publication.

Privacy Considerations

LDSO-Core does not define properties for personal data. However, instances of legal documents may contain personal data (names of parties, signatories, judicial case participants). Implementors MUST assess privacy obligations under applicable law (e.g., GDPR) before publishing legal document graphs that include personal information.
